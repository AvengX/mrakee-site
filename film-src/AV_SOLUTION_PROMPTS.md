# AV imagery — what the site now needs

The client's content changed the business. The site was built around
digital signage; MRAKEE is an **AV systems integration** company, and
digital signage is one of nine portfolios.

Every photograph currently on the site was commissioned for signage —
malls, airports, shop floors, menu boards. None of it shows a boardroom,
a classroom, a control room or an auditorium, which is most of what the
client actually sells. The pages work, but the pictures are arguing for
the old business.

## Rules

- **16:9 landscape.**
- **Solutions:** keep the bottom-right clear (Flow watermark, cropped off).
- **Industries:** keep the bottom-**left** clear too — the industry label
  sits there.
- **No real brand names or logos.** No legible on-screen text.

## Shared style block — append to every prompt

```
Photorealistic architectural interior photography, bright and airy, natural
daylight, premium contemporary materials in white, pale stone, glass and warm
timber. Full-frame camera at 24-35mm, deep focus, camera at standing eye
level. The AV technology is integrated into the architecture and is the focal
point, screens clearly lit and legible. Calm, uncluttered, high-end corporate.
A few people, unposed. No visible brand names, logos or trademarks. Minimal
on-screen text. 16:9 landscape.
```

## Solutions — the six needed

**01 — Video Conferencing**
> A modern mid-size meeting room mid-call, a large display on the end wall
> showing a simple video-call grid of participants, a ceiling microphone
> array and a table puck, three colleagues seated at the table.

**02 — Corporate Board Room**
> An executive boardroom with a long timber table, a very large display on
> the far wall, discreet ceiling microphones and recessed lighting, glass
> partition to a corridor, two people talking at one end.

**03 — Smart Classroom**
> A contemporary university teaching room with a large interactive display
> at the front and a digital lectern beside it, tiered or flat seating with
> students, daylight from a side window wall.

**04 — Command & Control Room**
> A control room with a wall of tiled displays showing simple abstract
> monitoring dashboards, a curved row of operator workstations facing it,
> two operators seated, calm even lighting — bright, not a dark bunker.

**05 — Experience Centre**
> A brand experience space with a large curved or tiled display wall
> showing an abstract immersive visual, a touch table in the foreground,
> polished floor, one visitor interacting.

**08 — Large Venue & Auditorium**
> A modern auditorium from the rear, raked seating, a very large projection
> or LED screen on stage showing a simple abstract slide, stage lighting and
> line-array speakers visible, partly filled audience.
